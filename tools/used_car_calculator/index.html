A complete workup on a used car before you buy it: what it is really worth, what it will cost you over the years you keep it, which loan length actually makes sense, and every open safety recall on record.

You only need four things — year, make and model, mileage and the asking price (* below), or just the VIN. Everything else has a sensible default and only sharpens the answer. Pick your state and the report adds your local taxes, fees, insurance levels and inspection rules. Nothing you enter leaves this browser.

Data sources

The report works fully offline from its built-in dataset. VIN decoding, recalls, complaints, crash ratings and EPA fuel economy come from free U.S. government APIs that need no key. These optional free keys add live prices and market context; they are kept in this browser only and sent straight to the provider.

History & market comparables

Comparables are the single biggest lever on accuracy. Pull one to three asking prices for the same year, trim and mileage from KBB, Edmunds, Cars.com or Carvana. Without them the price component of the score stays neutral rather than guessing.

    Pre-purchase checklist

    • Run the VIN through a history report (Carfax or AutoCheck).
    • Confirm every open recall above was actually repaired — free at any franchised dealer.
    • Independent pre-purchase inspection, about $100–200. A refusal is a walk-away.
    • Cold-start it: listen for ticking, knocking, rough idle; watch for smoke.
    • Check fluids — oil level and colour, coolant, brake, transmission. Milky oil means leave.
    • Look for mismatched paint, uneven panel gaps, overspray — all signs of body repair.
    • Check tyre wear is even; uneven wear points at alignment or suspension.
    • Test every electrical item: windows, locks, A/C hot and cold, screen, cameras, lights.
    • Drive at highway speed — vibration, pulling, gear hesitation, brake shudder.
    • Confirm the title is in the seller's name and the VIN matches dash and door jamb.
    • Get the out-the-door price in writing before discussing financing or a trade-in.
    • Decline dealer add-ons: VIN etching, nitrogen, paint protection are nearly pure margin.
    • Arrive with a credit-union pre-approval so the dealer has to beat a real number.
