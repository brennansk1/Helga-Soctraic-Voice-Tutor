## How this becomes an automated check

Concrete, mapped to files in this repo. Every threshold below is either sourced above or explicitly labelled as a local policy choice.

1. **Coverage gate — `services/core/course_builder.py` (SyllabusAuditor).**
   Replace the current flat 70% topic-coverage floor with the **CS2013 two-tier rule**, the only standards-body percentage available: mark each syllabus topic `core` or `elective`, then assert **100% of `core` topics present** and **≥80% of `elective` topics present**. Fail the build on a core miss; WARN on elective. Source: CS2023 Version Gamma, §Core Topics.
2. **Coverage must distinguish "mentioned" from "taught" — same file.**
   Implement Curriculum Cartographer's i/p/a rubric per concept: **`introduced`** (term appears in `Core Explanation`), **`practiced`** (appears in `Worked Example` or an exercise), **`assessed`** (appears in a `Socratic Hooks` question or a flashcard). Score **Complete** only on i∧p∧a. A topic that is merely `introduced` counts as **Weak**, not covered. This is the direct fix for the failure mode that paper documents — *"LLMs … struggle to distinguish between simply mentioning a concept and genuinely teaching it"* — and is exactly how a hollow course scores 100% on a naive checker.
3. **Bloom Ceiling per concept — `services/common/concept_doc.py`.**
   `L_max = max(Bloom level across the concept's hooks and exercises)`, Remember=1…Create=6. Gate a *lesson* on its ceiling, not each concept. See `QUALITY_rosenshine_bloom.md` for why this must use 3 tiers, not 6 levels.
4. **Readability as WARN only, never a gate — `services/common/concept_doc.py`.**
   Use `textstat.flesch_kincaid_grade()` against the **CCSS 2012 supplement's Flesch-Kincaid column** (grades 11–CCR = **FK 10.34–14.2**), because that mapping is itself published by a standards body and Lexile is proprietary with no free scorer. Prefer `textstat.dale_chall_readability_score()` for STEM, since New Dale-Chall did **not** saturate in the eLife study where FRE did. **Emit WARN, never FAIL** — Flesch's own paper says the scale under-rates above 7th grade, and 22% of modern science abstracts score below zero.
   **Do not define a "graduate" readability tier without labelling it as Helga's own extrapolation** — the Lexile construct stops at first-year college.
5. **Length band — `services/core/course_builder.py` (ContentHydrator).**
   Keep the 900–1600 word band, now with an external anchor: human-written STEM lessons average **1,744 words** (LessonBench-V1). But add a **countervailing** check from the Stanford JELS finding that hallucination scales with answer length: track **claims-per-doc**, not just words, and prefer splitting a concept over letting one doc grow.
6. **Factuality gate — new module, offline-compatible.**
   Run **MiniCheck-Flan-T5-L (770M, Apache-2.0)** or **HHEM-2.1-Open (0.1B, Apache-2.0)** locally as a single forward pass per (research-provided grounding doc, extracted claim). Both fit the Mac Mini offline constraint and reach GPT-4-level agreement at ~1/400th the cost. **Do NOT build an atomic-decomposition stage first** — it did not improve accuracy for any of 8 systems tested and doubled cost.
   **Gate on groundedness, not truth**: assert every `Key Facts` claim is supported by a retrieved source, because the only regulatory standard on record (Texas) is *zero errors by affidavit*, which is not an achievable rate target.
   **Set the threshold as an explicit local policy, not a cited one** — there is no published acceptable-error-rate for educational content.
7. **Reject the LLM judge for factuality scoring.** ~75% balanced accuracy is the field ceiling; 1 in 4 claim-level verdicts is wrong. Combined with the logged HelgaBench ±1.4/5 noise floor, never gate on a single judged run.

## Confidence and gaps

**High confidence (primary text extracted and read by me):** CS2013's 100%/80% rule; CS2013 Tier I/II hours and the 280→290→308 trend; CS2023's CS-Core/KA-Core definitions and the 268-hour / 17-KA total; the Curriculum Cartographer abstract numbers.

**Could NOT be sourced credibly — reported as gaps, not filled:**
- **No AACSB or regional-accreditor curriculum-coverage percentage exists** that I could find. These bodies use outcomes-evidence language, not topic-coverage percentages.
- **CS2023 itself states no coverage percentage.** It replaced CS2013's 80% rule with a binary CS-Core-is-mandatory model. If you want a percentage from a standards body, **CS2013's 80% is the only one, and it is the superseded document.** Say so when you cite it.
- **No published "errors per 1000 words" for professionally published textbooks.** The only regulatory anchor is Texas's "free from factual error," i.e. zero by affidavit.
- **No verified per-claim error rate for LLM-generated educational content at meaningful scale.** Best available are n=12 (16.67%) and an indirect app-review study. Helga's own measurement (`helga-content-factual-errors.md`) may be more specific than anything published.
- **No expert-rated comparison of AI-generated long-form concept PROSE vs textbook prose has been published.** Every AI-vs-human study in the literature compares *questions*, *objectives*, or *lesson plans*. Helga's exact artifact is unstudied.
- **No learner-outcome evaluation exists for ANY of the 11+ AI course-authoring vendors.** Category-wide vacuum; metrics are uniformly production-side or satisfaction-side.
- **No graduate-level Lexile exists.** Hard negative, not a search failure.
- **No published Lexile for scientific journal articles.**
- **Dale-Chall's 1995 cutoff table** is implementation-consistent but the primary book text was not verified (no DOI, no Crossref record, not online).
- **Blocked, not absent:** AAAS Project 2061 textbook evaluations (aaas.org 404, project2061.org 403); Texas Freedom Network reviewer error counts (404); MQM/LISA per-1000-word translation-QA thresholds (JS-rendered, PDFs 403).

**Sub-agent disagreement recorded above:** Curriculum Cartographer's ILO κ was reported as 0.577 by one agent; the abstract I extracted says 0.749 across six PBL courses. Only the verbatim figures are cited as verified.

**Tooling caveat worth remembering:** the WebFetch summarizer returned a **confidently wrong title** for the Curriculum Cartographer PDF (it reported an unrelated paper about repository anonymization). Local `pdftotext` extraction was required to establish the truth. Treat single-pass fetch summaries of PDFs as unreliable.
