# The Complete Utah K–12 Teachable Curriculum: A Concept-by-Concept Reference for the Helga Socratic Tutor

> Saved verbatim as the canonical curriculum-standards reference for the catalog build
> (Workstream B / B16). Source: research report supplied 2026-06-30. Standards sourced from
> the Utah State Board of Education (schools.utah.gov) and uen.org. Re-scrape at each USBE
> adoption cycle — math standards are mid-revision (2025–2026).

## TL;DR
- This document maps the **official Utah Core Standards** (adopted by the Utah State Board of Education; sourced from schools.utah.gov and uen.org) grade-by-grade and strand-by-strand for every subject realistically teachable through a text/voice/media Socratic dialogue chatbot: **Mathematics, Science (SEEd), English Language Arts, Social Studies, World Languages, Health (concepts), K-12 Computer Science (theory), Financial Literacy, and Library/Media & Digital Literacy.**
- Each subject lists the **mandatory standards floor** plus **★ supplementary enrichment concepts** that strengthen the subject beyond the state requirement; subjects requiring hands-on motor/studio/performance/lab skill (PE motor skills, instrumental/studio-art technique, behind-the-wheel driver's ed, lab-only skills) are **excluded or limited to their Socratic-teachable theory portions**.
- Utah uses **its own standards, not Common Core directly**: the integrated **Secondary Mathematics I/II/III** high-school pathway (not Algebra/Geometry), **SEEd** science, **P-12 ELA** (2023), **Social Studies**, ACTFL-based **World Languages** (2020), **Health** (2019), **Computer Science** (2019), and the graduation-required **General Financial Literacy** course.

## Key Findings

1. **Utah's standards are organized into "strands" (domains) containing numbered "standards"** across every subject. For Helga, the strand is the natural skill-tree branch and each numbered standard is a masterable node with multiple correct-answer paths.

2. **High school mathematics follows an integrated international model.** Per Utah's adopting districts citing USBE, "The Utah State Board of Education voted to restructure the core using an International Model… students will learn all Algebra and Geometry concepts as well as topics included in Algebra II and Pre Calculus if the student completes mathematics course work through Secondary Math III Honors." Instead of Algebra I → Geometry → Algebra II, Utah teaches Secondary Mathematics I, II, III, each blending Algebra, Functions, Geometry, Statistics/Probability, and Number.

3. **Science uses three-dimensional SEEd standards** (Science & Engineering Practices + Crosscutting Concepts + Disciplinary Core Ideas). The conceptual content is highly Socratic-teachable; only physical lab manipulation is excluded. High school science is course-based (Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Earth & Space Science) rather than grade-based.

4. **ELA (2023 revision) uses three strands** — Reading (Foundational Skills woven in), Writing, and Speaking & Listening — with Language woven throughout. Speaking & Listening pairs perfectly with Helga's TTS/STT.

5. **Several subjects are graduation requirements**: General Financial Literacy (0.5 credit, juniors/seniors), U.S. Government & Citizenship (0.5 credit plus a Basic Civics Test), plus the SEEd science and integrated math sequences.

6. **Excluded or partially excluded subjects**: Physical Education (motor skills), Fine Arts studio/performance technique, Driver Education behind-the-wheel, and Keyboarding (motor skill) — though the theory/concept portions of music theory, art history/criticism, health, and rules-of-the-road ARE Socratic-teachable and are flagged where relevant.

## Details

### Conventions used in this document
- **Baseline (no mark)** = official Utah Core Standard, the mandatory floor.
- **★** = supplementary enrichment concept recommended in addition to, but not part of, the Utah Core Standards.
- Standard codes (e.g., K.CC, 6.RP, SI.A.REI, BIO.1) are Utah's official strand codes.
- "Mathematical Practices" (MP) and the SEEd Science & Engineering Practices / Crosscutting Concepts are habits-of-mind strands present at every grade.

## SUBJECT 1: MATHEMATICS

USBE confirms: "The Utah State Board of Education adopted the K-12 Utah Core Standards for Mathematics in January 2016," and notes that "Currently P-12 Mathematics Standards are in the revision process." Every grade K–8 and every secondary course also includes the **Standards for Mathematical Practice (MP 1–8)**: (1) Make sense of problems and persevere; (2) Reason abstractly and quantitatively; (3) Construct viable arguments and critique reasoning of others; (4) Model with mathematics; (5) Use appropriate tools strategically; (6) Attend to precision; (7) Look for and make use of structure; (8) Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning.

### Kindergarten
- **Counting & Cardinality (K.CC):** count to 100 by ones and tens; count forward from a given number; write numerals 0–20; count to tell the number of objects (one-to-one correspondence, cardinality); compare quantities and compare written numerals.
- **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (K.OA):** represent/solve addition-subtraction word problems within 10; decompose numbers ≤10; make 10 from any number 1–9; fluently add/subtract within 5.
- **Number & Operations in Base Ten (K.NBT):** compose/decompose numbers 11–19 into tens and ones.
- **Measurement & Data (K.MD):** describe measurable attributes; directly compare two objects; classify objects into categories and count.
- **Geometry (K.G):** identify/describe shapes (squares, circles, triangles, rectangles, hexagons, cubes, cones, cylinders, spheres); positional words; 2D vs 3D; compose simple shapes.
- ★ Subitizing; ★ skip counting by 5s; ★ coin recognition.

### Grade 1
- **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (1.OA):** add/subtract within 20 word problems; properties of operations; relationship between addition and subtraction; fluency within 10; meaning of the equal sign; determine the unknown in an equation.
- **Number & Operations in Base Ten (1.NBT):** count to 120 from any number; place value (tens/ones); compare two-digit numbers; add within 100; mentally find 10 more/less; subtract multiples of 10.
- **Measurement & Data (1.MD):** compare/order lengths; tell/write time (hour, half-hour); represent/interpret data; values of pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters.
- **Geometry (1.G):** defining vs non-defining attributes; compose 2D and 3D shapes; partition into halves and fourths.
- ★ Counting by 2s; ★ fact families.

### Grade 2
- **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (2.OA):** add/subtract within 100; fluently add/subtract within 20; equal groups (odd/even, arrays).
- **Number & Operations in Base Ten (2.NBT):** three-digit place value; skip-count by 5s/10s/100s; read/write/compare numbers to 1000; add/subtract within 1000.
- **Measurement & Data (2.MD):** measure/estimate in inches, feet, centimeters, meters; time to five minutes; money ($ and ¢ symbols); line plots, picture/bar graphs.
- **Geometry (2.G):** shapes by attributes; partition rectangles into rows/columns; halves, thirds, fourths.
- ★ Multiplication as repeated addition; ★ elapsed time.

### Grade 3
- **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (3.OA):** multiplication/division within 100; two-step word problems; arithmetic patterns; fluently multiply/divide within 100.
- **Number & Operations in Base Ten (3.NBT):** round to nearest 10/100; add/subtract within 1000; multiply one-digit by multiples of 10.
- **Number & Operations—Fractions (3.NF):** fractions as numbers on a number line; equivalent fractions; compare fractions.
- **Measurement & Data (3.MD):** time to the minute, elapsed time; liquid volume and mass; scaled bar/picture graphs, line plots; area (unit squares, multiplication link); perimeter.
- **Geometry (3.G):** quadrilateral categories; partition shapes into equal-area unit fractions.
- ★ Order of operations; ★ multiplication-fact automaticity.

### Grade 4
- **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (4.OA):** multiplicative comparison; multistep problems; factors, multiples, prime/composite; generate/analyze patterns.
- **Number & Operations in Base Ten (4.NBT):** place value to 1,000,000; compare multi-digit numbers; multi-digit multiplication; division with remainders.
- **Number & Operations—Fractions (4.NF):** equivalence; compare; add/subtract fractions and mixed numbers; multiply fraction by whole number; decimal notation (tenths, hundredths); compare decimals.
- **Measurement & Data (4.MD):** convert units; area/perimeter; line plots with fractions; angles and measurement (protractor).
- **Geometry (4.G):** points, lines, rays, angles, perpendicular/parallel; classify two-dimensional figures; lines of symmetry.
- ★ Coordinate plane; ★ long-division fluency.

### Grade 5
- **Operations & Algebraic Thinking (5.OA):** numerical expressions with parentheses/brackets; analyze patterns and ordered pairs.
- **Number & Operations in Base Ten (5.NBT):** powers of 10; decimals to thousandths; multiply multi-digit whole numbers fluently; divide; decimal operations to hundredths.
- **Number & Operations—Fractions (5.NF):** add/subtract unlike denominators; multiply fractions; fractions as division; divide unit fractions by whole numbers.
- **Measurement & Data (5.MD):** convert within systems; line plots; volume (unit cubes, prism formulas).
- **Geometry (5.G):** first-quadrant coordinate plane; classify figures in hierarchies.
- ★ Negative numbers on the number line; ★ four-quadrant graphing.

### Grade 6
- **Ratios & Proportional Relationships (6.RP):** ratio language; unit rates; percent as rate per 100.
- **The Number System (6.NS):** divide fractions by fractions; fluently compute with multi-digit numbers/decimals; GCF and LCM; rational/negative numbers; four-quadrant plane; absolute value.
- **Expressions & Equations (6.EE):** exponents; algebraic expressions; equivalent expressions; solve one-variable equations and inequalities; dependent/independent variables.
- **Geometry (6.G):** area of triangles/quadrilaterals; volume with fractional edges; polygons on the coordinate plane; surface area via nets.
- **Statistics & Probability (6.SP):** variability; measures of center and spread; box plots, histograms, dot plots.
- ★ Introductory probability; ★ scientific-notation preview.

### Grade 7
- **Ratios & Proportional Relationships (7.RP):** unit rates with fractions; constant of proportionality; percent problems (tax, tip, discount, interest, markup, percent change).
- **The Number System (7.NS):** operations with rational numbers including integers; terminating/repeating decimals.
- **Expressions & Equations (7.EE):** factor/expand linear expressions; solve px + q = r and px + q > r.
- **Geometry (7.G):** scale drawings; constructions; cross-sections; circle area/circumference; angle relationships; area/volume/surface area.
- **Statistics & Probability (7.SP):** random sampling and inferences; comparative inferences; probability models; compound events.
- ★ Two-step inequalities on the number line; ★ probability simulations.

### Grade 8
- **The Number System (8.NS):** rational vs irrational; approximate irrationals.
- **Expressions & Equations (8.EE):** integer exponents; roots; scientific notation; slope; linear equations (incl. no/infinite solutions); systems.
- **Functions (8.F):** define/evaluate/compare functions; linear vs nonlinear; model relationships; describe from graphs.
- **Geometry (8.G):** transformations; congruence and similarity; parallel-line angles; Pythagorean Theorem and converse; volume of cones, cylinders, spheres.
- **Statistics & Probability (8.SP):** scatter plots, line of best fit, two-way tables.
- ★ Function notation; ★ exponential growth preview.

### Secondary Mathematics I (integrated)
HS courses include MP 1–8; Utah marks modeling opportunities with a star (🟊).
- **Number & Quantity (N.Q):** quantities, units, precision in modeling.
- **Algebra (A.SSE, A.CED, A.REI):** linear/exponential expressions; create/solve linear equations and inequalities; systems.
- **Functions (F.IF, F.BF, F.LE):** function notation; rate of change; build linear/exponential functions; compare growth.
- **Geometry (G.CO, G.GPE):** transformations and congruence; constructions; coordinate geometry.
- **Statistics (S.ID):** one/two-variable data; linear models; correlation.
- ★ Recursive sequences; ★ piecewise functions.

### Secondary Mathematics II (integrated)
- **Number & Quantity (N.RN, N.CN):** rational exponents; complex numbers and operations.
- **Algebra (A.SSE, A.APR, A.CED, A.REI):** quadratic/polynomial expressions and equations; systems.
- **Functions (F.IF, F.BF, F.TF):** quadratic functions and features; transformations; intro trigonometry/unit circle.
- **Geometry (G.SRT, G.C, G.GMD):** dilations and similarity; right-triangle trigonometry; circle theorems; volume (Cavalieri's principle).
- **Statistics & Probability (S.CP):** conditional probability and independence.
- ★ Completing-the-square derivations; ★ quadratic-formula proofs.

### Secondary Mathematics III (integrated)
- **Number & Quantity (N.CN):** conjugates, moduli; rectangular and polar form [Honors].
- **Algebra (A.APR, A.SSE, A.CED, A.REI):** polynomial operations, Remainder Theorem; zeros and factors; rational expressions; geometric series (e.g., mortgage payments).
- **Functions (F.IF, F.BF, F.TF):** polynomial, rational, radical functions; composition/inverses; extend trigonometric functions; model periodic phenomena.
- **Geometry (G.SRT, G.GMD):** Laws of Sines and Cosines; modeling with geometry.
- **Statistics & Probability (S.IC):** inference; sampling, experiments, simulations.
- ★ Logarithmic functions; ★ matrices.

### Beyond the core (★ / Utah supplemental courses)
- ★ Precalculus (identities, vectors, conics, sequences/series, polar coordinates, limits); ★ Introductory Statistics (Utah supplemental); ★ Mathematics of Personal Finance / Modern Mathematics / Mathematical Decision Making for Life (Utah supplemental); ★ Calculus.

## SUBJECT 2: SCIENCE — Utah SEEd (Science with Engineering Education) Standards

Every grade/course embeds **Science & Engineering Practices** (ask questions/define problems; develop/use models; plan/carry out investigations; analyze/interpret data; use mathematics & computational thinking; construct explanations/design solutions; engage in argument from evidence; obtain/evaluate/communicate information) and **Crosscutting Concepts** (patterns; cause and effect; scale/proportion/quantity; systems and system models; energy and matter; structure and function; stability and change). Hands-on lab manipulation is excluded from Helga; the conceptual/analytical content is Socratic-teachable.

### Elementary (K–5)
- **Kindergarten:** K.1 Weather Patterns; K.2 Living Things and Their Surroundings; K.3 Forces, Motion, and Interactions. ★ Seasonal journaling; ★ animal classification.
- **Grade 1:** 1.1 Light and Sound; 1.2 Living Things—Survival and Traits; 1.3 Patterns in the Sky. ★ Shadows; ★ inherited vs learned traits.
- **Grade 2:** 2.1 Properties of Matter; 2.2 Living Things and Their Habitats; 2.3 Earth's Processes (changes to land). ★ States of matter; ★ erosion modeling.
- **Grade 3:** 3.1 Weather and Climate; 3.2 Effects of Traits and the Environment; 3.3 Force Affects Motion. ★ Food chains; ★ fossils as evidence.
- **Grade 4:** 4.1 Organisms Functioning in Their Environment; 4.2 Energy Transfer; 4.3 Wave Patterns; 4.4 Observable Patterns in the Geosphere. ★ Renewable/nonrenewable energy; ★ electromagnetic spectrum.
- **Grade 5:** 5.1 Changes to Earth Over Time (Earth's systems); 5.2 Properties of Matter (particles, conservation); 5.3 Cycling of Matter and Flow of Energy (ecosystems). ★ Water cycle detail; ★ human ecosystem impact.

### Middle (6–8)
- **Grade 6:** 6.1 Structure and Motion Within the Solar System (gravity, Sun-Earth-Moon, seasons/phases/eclipses); 6.2 Energy Affects Matter (heat, states, density); 6.3 Earth's Weather and Climate Patterns (water cycle, circulation, greenhouse effect); 6.4 Stability and Change in Ecosystems. ★ Exoplanets; ★ climate change depth.
- **Grade 7:** 7.1 Forces Are Interactions Between Matter (Newton's 1st/2nd Laws conceptual, collisions); 7.2 Changes to Earth Over Time (rock cycle, Earth's interior, plate tectonics, geologic time, relative dating); 7.3 Structure and Function of Life (cells, levels of organization); 7.4 Reproduction and Inheritance (sexual/asexual variation, adaptations, natural selection, fossil record). ★ Punnett squares; ★ Newton's Third Law.
- **Grade 8:** 8.1 Matter and Energy Interact (atoms/molecules, conservation, synthetic materials); 8.2 Energy Is Stored and Transferred (energy/mass/velocity, light and mechanical waves); 8.3 Cycling of Matter in Ecosystems (photosynthesis, cellular respiration); 8.4 Interactions with Natural Systems and Resources (renewable/nonrenewable, climate, natural disasters/mitigation). ★ Balancing chemical equations; ★ wave-particle duality.

### High School courses
- **Biology:** BIO.1 Interactions with Organisms and the Environment; BIO.2 Structure and Function of Life; BIO.3 Genetic Patterns; BIO.4 Effects of Evolution (Change Over Time). ★ Ecology field-study discussion; ★ CRISPR/biotech ethics.
- **Chemistry:** CHEM.1 The Structure and Properties of Atoms; CHEM.2 The Structure and Properties of Molecules; CHEM.3 Stability and Change in Chemical Systems; CHEM.4 Energy (conservation/transfer). ★ Acid-base chemistry; ★ nuclear chemistry.
- **Physics:** PHYS.1 Forces and Interactions (Motion); PHYS.2 Energy; PHYS.3 Fields; PHYS.4 Waves. ★ Special relativity intro; ★ circuits.
- **Earth & Space Science:** ESS.1 Matter and Energy in Space; ESS.2 Patterns in Earth's History and Processes; ESS.3 System Interactions—Atmosphere, Hydrosphere, and Geosphere; ESS.4 Stability and Change in Natural Resources. ★ Utah-specific geology (Great Salt Lake, Wasatch Fault); ★ cosmology.
- **Supplemental electives (★ Utah Supplemental SEEd):** Astronomy, Botany, Environmental Science, Wildlife Biology, Zoology, Genetics, Geology, Human Anatomy & Physiology, Marine Biology/Oceanography (lab-only components excluded).

## SUBJECT 3: ENGLISH LANGUAGE ARTS (Utah P-12 ELA Standards, adopted April 2023)

Three strands at every grade: **Reading (R)** [Reading Literature (RL), Reading Informational (RI), and Foundational Skills in primary grades], **Writing (W)**, and **Speaking & Listening (SL)**, with **Language** woven throughout. SL and read-aloud foundational skills are an ideal match for Helga's TTS/STT.

- **Foundational reading (K–5):** print concepts; phonological/phonemic awareness (explicitly added in grades 2–3 in the 2023 revision); phonics & word recognition (letter-sound correspondences, decoding, syllabication, morphology/roots/affixes, etymology — basic phonics mastered by grade 3); fluency.
- **Reading strand:** Key Ideas & Details (main idea/theme, summarize, cite evidence, inference); Craft & Structure (literal/figurative/connotative word meaning, text structure, point of view/purpose, author's geographic location/identity in upper grades); Integration of Knowledge & Ideas (evaluate arguments/claims/evidence, compare texts, illustrations/media); Range & Complexity (grade-level literature/informational incl. seminal U.S. documents, Lexile bands). ★ Reading logs; ★ literature circles; ★ close-reading protocols.
- **Writing strand:** opinion/argumentative, informative/explanatory, narrative; research-plan-draft-revise-edit-publish (reading-writing connection); conventions; manuscript and cursive (e.g., grade 5). ★ Multi-paragraph essays with thesis development; ★ MLA/APA basics (secondary); ★ creative writing.
- **Speaking & Listening strand:** participate in conversations/collaborations; acknowledge/respond to perspectives; present with logical sequencing and clear pronunciation; integrate/evaluate media. ★ Formal debate; ★ Socratic seminar; ★ oral storytelling.
- **Secondary bands:** Grade 6; Grades 7–8; Grades 9–10; Grades 11–12, escalating the three strands (e.g., 11-12.R.14 analyze texts of literary significance across time periods; 11-12.W.1 write arguments using logical reasoning and sufficient evidence).
- ★ **Utah Secondary Supplemental ELA courses:** Journalism, Speech/Debate, Creative Writing/Literary Magazine, Humanities, Professional & Technical Communication.

## SUBJECT 4: SOCIAL STUDIES (Utah Core Standards for Social Studies)

K–6 standards rest on four disciplines — **History, Geography, Civics, Economics** — plus K–2 Financial Literacy (strands: Culture, Citizenship, Geography, Financial Literacy). The 3–6 framework rests on four essential understandings: human rights & responsibilities; global interconnectedness; systems of power/authority/governance; continuity and change over time.

- **Kindergarten:** Culture (similar/different individuals/families; elements of culture — language, dress, food, shelter, stories); Citizenship (rules/consequences, respect, safety, national symbols/songs); Geography (directions, maps/globes, land/water features); Financial Literacy (needs vs wants, why people work).
- **Grade 1:** good citizenship via historical/literary examples (Washington, MLK); school rules/safety; family/community roles; geography; economic choices.
- **Grade 2:** community government/services; conflict resolution; why towns need governments; local economic institutions; saving; scarcity/opportunity cost.
- **Grade 3:** Strand 1 Your Community (geography, defining culture, interdependence/specialization, environmental adaptation); Strand 2 Rights and Responsibilities (intro to U.S. Constitution, service project); Strand 3 Connecting Your Community to the World (compare cultures, mapping).
- **Grade 4 (Utah, elementary):** Strand 1 geography & the five historic tribes (Goshute, Navajo, Paiute, Shoshone, Ute); Strand 2 settlement/development (Utah War, Bear River Massacre, Black Hawk War, mining, railroads); Strand 3 modern era (Cold War, civil rights, Utah Constitution Article I, symbols); Strand 4 A New Millennium (2000–present).
- **Grade 5 (U.S. history, elementary):** Strand 1 geography & human interaction in North America (500+ tribes); eras of U.S. history; civics; limited government, state vs federal.
- **Grade 6 (world history/geography):** Strand 1 early civilizations (early governments — monarchy, oligarchy, tyranny, pure democracy, republic, theocracy); Strand 2 post-classical era (world religions — Buddhism, Christianity, Confucianism, Hinduism, Islam, Judaism, Sikhism, Taoism); Strand 3 colonization/imperialism/revolution; Strand 4 modern world (Cold War, current events).

### Secondary courses (7–12)
- **Grade 7 — Utah Studies:** UT Strand 1 Native Innovations and Adaptations (Prehistory–Ca. 1847); UT 2 Utah's Diverse Peoples (Ca. 1847–1896); UT 3 Utah in the Union (Ca. 1896–1945); UT 4 Utah in the World (Ca. 1945–2002); UT 5 Looking Towards Utah's Future (Ca. 2003–Present).
- **United States History I:** Strand 1 Three Worlds Meet; 2 Colonization; 3 The American Revolution; 4 The U.S. Constitution; 5 Development of Political Institutions and Processes; 6 Expansion; 7 The Civil War and Reconstruction.
- **United States History II:** Strand 1 Industrialization; 2 Reform Movements; 3 Emergence as a World Power (imperialism, WWI); 4 Traditions and Social Change; 5 Economic Boom, Bust, and the Role of Government; 6 Another Global Conflict and the Beginnings of the Cold War (WWII); 7 The Cold War Era and a Changing America; 8 The 21st Century United States.
- **World History (9–12):** WH Strand 1 Prehistory to the Neolithic Revolution; 2 Classical Civilizations; 3 An Age of Expanding Connections; 4 Global Exchange and Colonization; 5 Revolutions, Industrialization, and Empires; 6 20th-Century Global Conflicts; 7 Globalization and the Contemporary World.
- **World Geography:** physical systems/natural resources; population distribution and migration; culture (religion, language, ethnicity); political systems; economic systems/development.
- **U.S. Government & Citizenship (0.5 credit, graduation-required, recommended for seniors):** Strand 1 Foundational Principles; 2 Civil Liberties, Civil Rights, and Responsibilities; 3 Distribution of Power (three branches, federalism, tribal governments, judicial review, how a bill becomes law); 4 Fiscal Policies and Decisions; 5 The U.S. and Our Relationship to the World. Also required: passing a **Basic Civics Test**. Per USBE guidelines (schools.utah.gov), the test draws 50 questions from the USCIS list of 100, and "Students must correctly answer a minimum of 35 of the 50 questions to pass the test" (required under Utah Code 53A-13-109.5 / USBE Rule R277-700-8); AP U.S. Government may substitute.
- **Supplementary (electives, Socratic-teachable):** ★ Economics, ★ Psychology, ★ Sociology, ★ Anthropology, ★ Geography II, ★ Ethnic Studies, ★ World Civilizations.

## SUBJECT 5: WORLD LANGUAGES (Utah World Languages Core Standards for Proficiency, adopted February 2020)

Based on ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines (2012) and the National Standards' "5 C's." Organized by **proficiency level** (Novice Low/Mid/High → Intermediate Low/Mid/High → Advanced) using "I can" statements — an exceptional fit for Helga's adaptive, mastery-through-multiple-correct-answers model with TTS/STT.

Goal areas / strands:
- **Communication** (the core standard, blending the others): **Interpretive** (listening + reading); **Interpersonal** (two-way speaking+listening or writing+reading); **Presentational** (writing, speaking, visually representing).
- **Cultures** (blended with Communication); **Connections, Comparisons, Communities** (embedded within Communication).

"I can" indicators by level:
- **Novice:** recognize memorized words/phrases; exchange personal info (name, nationality, family, address); identify familiar things in pictures; short social interactions.
- **Intermediate:** create with language; handle uncomplicated tasks/social situations; narrate and describe in major time frames.
- ★ **Advanced (enrichment):** understand main ideas and most details on general/professional topics; follow stories across time frames.
- ★ Seal of Biliteracy preparation; ★ explicit grammar/conjugation systems; ★ cultural-products deep dives; ★ target-language literary reading. STT supports pronunciation; physical/cultural performance components are excluded.

## SUBJECT 6: HEALTH EDUCATION (Utah Health Education Core Standards, adopted April 2019)

Conceptual portions are Socratic-teachable; CPR/AED hands-on skill is excluded (only the knowledge component is teachable). Strands K–12 (grade-appropriate inclusion):
- **Strand 1: Health Foundations and Protective Factors of Healthy Self (HF):** trusted adults; safe/healthy relationships; goal-setting, decision-making, communication, resiliency.
- **Strand 2: Mental and Emotional Health (MEH):** identifying emotions; calming strategies (deep breathing, mindfulness); stress management; suicide and bullying prevention.
- **Strand 3: Safety and Disease Prevention (SDP):** injury prevention; first-aid knowledge; disease transmission/prevention (introduced after K).
- **Strand 4: Substance Abuse Prevention (SAP):** alcohol, tobacco, vaping, drugs; refusal skills; addiction cycle (introduced after K).
- **Strand 5: Nutrition (N):** food groups; healthy choices; fueling the body; food and culture; dietary guidelines.
- **Strand 6: Human Development (HD):** hygiene; appropriate vs inappropriate touch; adolescent brain development; reproductive anatomy/physiology; conception, fetal development, pregnancy and prevention; healthy relationships; refusal/consent; harassment/abuse prevention and reporting. Utah Code requires parental notification for child-sexual-abuse-prevention instruction and parental consent for sex education; instruction stresses abstinence before marriage and fidelity after.
- ★ Sleep hygiene; ★ digital wellness/screen-time balance; ★ media literacy on body image; ★ body-systems overview.

## SUBJECT 7: K-12 COMPUTER SCIENCE (Utah K-12 CS Standards, 2019; derived from the K-12 CS Framework)

Theory and concepts are highly Socratic-teachable. Built on **6 Core Concepts** and **7 Core Practices**, organized K-5 and 6-12.
- **6 Core Concepts (strands):** (1) Computing Systems (hardware/software, troubleshooting); (2) Networks and the Internet (connectivity, cybersecurity, secure communication); (3) Data and Analysis (collection, storage, visualization, interpretation); (4) Algorithms and Programming (instructions, loops, conditionals, variables, debugging — conceptual/pseudocode strongly Socratic-teachable); (5) Impacts of Computing (societal effects, design/equity, safe/responsible use); (6) Computational Thinking (K-5 emphasis, expected mastered by grade 6 — decomposition, abstraction, pattern recognition).
- **7 Core Practices:** fostering an inclusive computing culture; collaborating; recognizing/defining computational problems; developing/using abstractions; creating computational artifacts; testing/refining; communicating about computing (incl. intellectual-property/attribution).
- **Safe Computing:** privacy risks; protecting resources (password strength); cybersecurity concepts (malware — adware, trojan, virus, ransomware; social engineering — phishing).
- ★ AP CS Principles; ★ binary/number systems; ★ Boolean logic; ★ history of computing; ★ AI/ML conceptual literacy; ★ AI/data ethics.

## SUBJECT 8: GENERAL FINANCIAL LITERACY (Utah graduation requirement; 0.5 credit, grades 11–12; Core Code 01.00.00.00.100)

Per the Utah Office of State Treasurer: "In 2004, the Utah Board of Education approved a one-semester financial literacy class (General Financial Literacy) that is required for graduating students, beginning with the class of 2008, making Utah the first state to legislate and implement a financial literacy high school graduation mandate" (enabled by 2003's S.B. 154, sponsor Thomas V. Hatch). Per The Nation's Report Card: the requirement "went into effect for the graduating class of 2008," and 2008 S.B. 61 (sponsors Pat Jones and Karen Morgan) extended Financial and Economic Education to grades K-12 (delivered via the Financial and Economic Literacy Passport integrated across Math, Social Studies, and ELA).

The course has an end-of-course assessment mandated by Utah Code 53E-3-505 and Board Rule R277-704 (delivered via YouScience); per schools.utah.gov, "To align with national norms, the cut score for passing the assessment has been changed to 74%" (passing the assessment is not itself required for graduation — completing the course is).

Strands:
- **Strand 1: Economic concepts and economic thinking** — GDP, inflation, supply and demand, scarcity, factors of production, macro/microeconomics; economic systems (capitalism, laissez-faire, social market, socialism, fascism, communism; command/market/mixed); property rights and prosperity.
- **Strand 2: Personal financial priorities and rational decisions** — emotional/cultural/social influences; cost-benefit analysis; saving vs investing vs gambling; income sources (salary, wages, commissions, tips); entrepreneurship; gross/net/taxable income; W-2/W-4/I-9; post-secondary/career preparation; charitable giving.
- **Strand 3: Money management, financial institutions, saving & investing** — checking/debit/savings accounts; credit unions/NCUA; risk and return; time value of money (compound interest); risk management (acceptance, transfer, reduction); insurance and beneficiaries.
- **Strand 4: Credit and debt** — purpose/role of credit; creditworthiness (character, capacity, capital, collateral, conditions); types of credit (unsecured/secured, credit cards, installment, revolving, student loans, mortgage, payday/predatory); simple interest; credit reports/scores; co-signers/collateral; financial scams.
- ★ Budgeting simulations; ★ retirement accounts (401(k), IRA); ★ tax-filing walkthroughs; ★ cryptocurrency/digital-asset literacy.

## SUBJECT 9: LIBRARY MEDIA & DIGITAL LITERACY (Utah Library Media Core Standards)

Information and media literacy are highly Socratic-teachable. Three clusters (K-5 and 6-12):
- **Reading Engagement:** reading for lifelong intellectual, personal, and emotional growth; comprehend literary and informational texts; connect with libraries.
- **Information and Research** (Big6-aligned multi-step process): defining an information problem and identifying information needed; locating sources/accessing information (library layout, classification systems, catalogs, browsers, databases; primary vs secondary, scholarly vs popular); engaging with/extracting information (main ideas, supporting details, bias, point of view, conflicting information); organizing/synthesizing; creating new understandings.
- **Media Literacy:** evaluate messages for accuracy, authenticity, relevance, source authority; producing and presenting (intent, content, audience, length; quality criteria); **Digital Citizenship** (safe/responsible use; consequences of misusing media).
- ★ Lateral reading / source triangulation; ★ detecting misinformation/deepfakes; ★ citation ethics and plagiarism avoidance.

- **Hands-on-only CTE** (welding, culinary, cosmetology, automotive) — **excluded**; conceptual/safety/theory components partially teachable.

## Recommendations

1. **Build Helga's content tree using Utah's strand → standard hierarchy as the skill graph.** Each strand is a branch; each numbered standard is a masterable node with multiple correct-answer paths. Begin with the subjects whose standards are most granular and Socratic-friendly: **K-8 Mathematics, K-12 ELA, and General Financial Literacy.**

2. **Stage the build by leverage and testability.**
   - *Phase 1 (highest ROI):* K-8 Math; K-12 ELA (especially Speaking & Listening via TTS/STT); the graduation-required General Financial Literacy and U.S. Government & Citizenship courses — all mandatory and heavily assessed (RISE, Utah Aspire Plus, the GFL YouScience end-of-course exam with its 74% cut score, and the Basic Civics Test with its 35-of-50 passing threshold).
   - *Phase 2:* SEEd Science K-8 and the four high-school sciences (concepts), Social Studies K-12, Computer Science theory.
   - *Phase 3:* World Languages (the proficiency-level "I can" model maps perfectly onto a Duolingo-style mastery system plus Helga's Socratic layer), Health concepts, and Library/Digital Literacy.

3. **Pull exact standard text from the official sources** (schools.utah.gov PDFs and uen.org per-grade course pages, which give clean per-strand lists). Tag every node as **baseline** vs **★ enrichment** so educators/parents can toggle "core only" vs "core + enrichment."

4. **Exploit Helga's modalities per subject:** TTS/STT for ELA Speaking & Listening, World Languages, and phonics; media display for SEEd phenomena, geography maps, math diagrams, and art/music theory; Bloom's-taxonomy tracking to escalate from recall (e.g., "define inflation") to analysis/evaluation (e.g., "evaluate predatory lending") within each standard.

5. **Respect Utah legal/policy constraints in Health Strand 6 (Human Development):** build parental-notification/consent gating and abstinence-stressing framing into any sex-education content to remain Utah Code-compliant; otherwise the health concepts are fully teachable.

6. **Benchmarks that would change this plan:** Utah is **currently revising its P-12 Mathematics standards** (the January-2016 standards remain in force until adoption of the revision) and maintains a rolling revision timeline for all subjects. **Re-scrape the standards at each USBE adoption cycle.** If a subject's standards shift toward performance/portfolio assessment (less Socratic-amenable), deprioritize automated coverage there.

## Caveats
- **Standards are a floor, not curriculum.** Utah repeatedly states a standard "does not dictate curriculum — it only represents a proficiency level." Helga should teach *toward* the standards; specific sequencing/examples are design choices.
- **Some strand titles are summarized rather than verbatim** where official snippets did not isolate the exact capitalized heading (notably some World Geography strands and a few World History titles); confirm against the source PDFs before publication — the *content* under each is accurate.
- **Math standards are mid-revision (2025–2026).** The integrated Secondary I/II/III pathway and the 2016 K-12 standards described are current as of June 2026; verify before final build.
- **This document is not exhaustive to the individual lettered sub-standard level** (every "a/b/c/d" bullet). For a true node-by-node build, ingest the full official PDFs/UEN course pages per grade, which contain every lettered sub-standard. This reference captures every subject, grade, strand, and the representative concept set within each.
- **Excluded subjects (PE, studio/performance arts, driver's-ed behind-the-wheel, keyboarding, hands-on CTE)** are omitted by design per the Socratic-suitability filter; their teachable theory fragments are flagged ★ but not exhaustively enumerated.
- **Fine Arts and PE do have full Utah Core Standards;** they are intentionally minimized here because the core of those subjects is non-Socratic. If Helga later supports richer interactivity, the theory strands can be added.
